# Corporate GHG Emissions: Business Findings & Recommendations

**Reporting period:** 2024–2025  
**Data source:** Simulated Corporate Emissions Dataset  
**Unit:** tCO₂e

## 1. Key Findings

### Finding 1 — Annual GHG Emissions Declined

Reported GHG emissions decreased by **17.66%**, from **481.00 tCO₂e in 2024** to **396.04 tCO₂e in 2025**. However, further analysis is needed to identify which Scopes contributed to this decline and whether the reporting boundaries were consistent between years.

### Finding 2 — Scope 3 Dominated Total Emissions

Scope 3 accounted for **354.60 tCO₂e**, representing **89.54%** of total reported emissions in 2025. This suggests that the company should further examine its major Scope 3 emission sources to identify potential reduction priorities.

### Finding 3 — Purchased Steel Was the Largest Emission Source

Purchased steel generated **342.00 tCO₂e** in 2025, accounting for **96.45% of reported Scope 3 emissions** and approximately **86.35% of total emissions**. This concentration makes purchased steel a priority for further investigation into potential emissions reduction opportunities.

## 2. Business Risks and Data Limitations

### Risk 1 — Inconsistent Activity Coverage

The dataset includes emissions of **30.00 tCO₂e** from a natural gas boiler in 2024, but no corresponding record was found in 2025. This may affect the comparability of reported emissions between years and the interpretation of the **17.66%** decline. Further verification is needed to determine whether the activity was discontinued or omitted.

### Risk 2 — Emissions Data Inconsistency

A discrepancy of **10.00 tCO₂e** was identified in the 2024 purchased steel record. Recorded emissions were **390.00 tCO₂e**, compared with **380.00 tCO₂e** calculated from activity data and the emission factor. This discrepancy may affect the 2024 emissions total and the reported year-over-year change.

## 3. Business Recommendation

The company should prioritize investigating purchased steel emissions while improving the reliability of its emissions data. The next step is to verify the underlying activity data, emission factors, and reporting boundaries before evaluating the cost, feasibility, and potential benefits of sourcing lower-emission steel.

---

**Data note:** This assessment uses simulated data with limited Scope 3 coverage. Emission-factor units and sources have not been independently verified. Findings should not be interpreted as evidence of actual corporate emissions reduction performance.
